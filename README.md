# Social Media Executive Dashboard

### Revenue Realization: From Social Reach to Customer Value

An executive-focused Power BI report that brings together social audience size, engagement, brand awareness, and sales revenue to support strategic performance reviews. The dashboard enables decision-makers to compare company segments and explore whether stronger social media presence aligns with stronger business outcomes.

## Executive Overview

A large social audience does not, on its own, demonstrate commercial success. This report places audience and engagement indicators alongside revenue, helping executives evaluate performance in context rather than relying on follower counts alone.

The executive page supports three priorities:

- **Assess performance:** Review audience size, brand awareness, engagement, and revenue in a consolidated view.
- **Compare segments:** Explore differences across industries, company sizes, locations, and follower tiers.
- **Identify areas for investigation:** Highlight segments where audience scale and business outcomes appear misaligned.

## Business Questions

- Which industries have the strongest average brand awareness?
- How does sales revenue vary across follower tiers?
- Do larger follower bases coincide with stronger brand awareness?
- How does engagement differ across companies grouped by founding year?
- What share of total followers does a selected segment represent relative to industry benchmarks?

## Key Insights
At first glance, the scale looks impressive:
* 335M total followers
* Strong brand awareness score 5.86
* Yet engagement rate sits at just 2.09%
* And total revenue stands at $5.73K
The funnel is not just inefficient; it is leaking at multiple stages. 

🔍 What the Data Revealed
1. Reach is not translating into revenue;
There is no strong correlation between audience size and revenue generation. High reach does not guarantee commercial impact; signalling a breakdown between awareness and conversion.
2. Engagement is structurally declining;
Engagement peaked over a decade ago and has since stabilised at significantly lower levels. This suggests "content fatigue" and misalignment with current audience behaviour.
3. Bigger audiences are not more profitable;
The most followed “Mega” segment underperforms relative to “Large” players, who generate the highest revenue.
➡️ This indicates resource misallocation and over-reliance on vanity metrics.
4. Market opportunities are unevenly distributed;
While 'Technology and Industrial' sectors dominate social presence, industries like E-commerce remain under-leveraged, presenting clear expansion opportunities.
5. High-performing outliers exist;
Some smaller players achieve strong awareness and revenue simultaneously.
➡️ The strategy is working, just not being applied consistently.

⚠️ The Core Issue
The current strategy is heavily skewed towards top-of-funnel metrics (followers, reach) with insufficient emphasis on conversion efficiency and revenue attribution.

💡 What Should Change.
Management should;
* Shift focus from audience volume → audience value
* Re-allocate investment towards high-performing (Large) segments
* Modernise content strategy to reverse engagement decline
* Analyse and replicate top-performing outliers
* Introduce end-to-end funnel tracking (Engagement → Conversion → Revenue)

📌 Bottom Line
A large audience is only an asset if it converts to business value.
The priority now is clear: move from measuring attention to driving outcomes.
